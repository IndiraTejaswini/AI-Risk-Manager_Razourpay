## 1. What the model predicts

The estimand is **P(fails | ships)** - the probability an order fails to reach `delivered`, *given that it was handed to a carrier*. It is not P(fails).

The risk set is `order_delivered_carrier_date IS NOT NULL`. Orders that never shipped are removed from the population, not scored as negatives.

Return-to-origin is a post-shipment event by definition. An order cancelled in the warehouse, or whose stock turns out `unavailable`, cannot return to origin - it never survived to the point where the risk exists. Scoring those orders as negatives would put rows in the denominator that were never at risk, understating prevalence and rewarding the model for assigning low risk to outcomes it neither observes nor can act on.

On this panel the restriction removes **1,775 of 99,433 matured orders** (1.785%), of which **225** fall in the test window.

The 2 `delivered` rows are a data quirk, not a modelling choice: they reached the customer but carry no carrier date. They would have been negatives on either target, so removing them shifts no positive count; they are listed rather than silently dropped.

### What conditioning costs

`order_delivered_carrier_date` is a **post-checkout** field. Conditioning the population on it means selecting on something not knowable at scoring time, so:

- The deployed score is valid *conditional on shipment*. Unconditional risk is P(ships) x P(fails | ships); a consumer needing the unconditional quantity must multiply by a shipment probability this repo does not model.
- Selection into the risk set is not random - orders leave for reasons (seller-side cancellation, stock unavailability) that plausibly correlate with the covariates. This is conditioning on a post-treatment variable. It does not invalidate the predictive claim on the subpopulation, which is what the score is used for, but it does bar a causal reading of the coefficients.

The column is used for this and nothing else. It is excluded from every feature matrix, enforced behaviourally in `tests/test_leakage.py`; see `data/COLUMN_WHITELIST.md`.

## 3. The sample-size trade-off

Minimum detectable PR-AUC difference at each target's test positive count, on the approximation stated in `eval/positive_counts.md` section 6 (alpha = 0.05 two-sided, power = 0.80, paired at rho = 0.8).

| Assumed AP | PRIMARY (n_pos = 154) | SECONDARY (n_pos = 379) | Ratio |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.05 | 0.031 | 0.020 | 1.57x |
| 0.10 | 0.043 | 0.027 | 1.57x |
| 0.20 | 0.057 | 0.036 | 1.57x |
| 0.30 | 0.065 | 0.042 | 1.57x |

**This is the cost of the primary target, stated as a number.** A model comparison on the primary label needs a gap **1.57x larger** to resolve than the same comparison on the secondary. At AP = 0.10 that is 0.043 against 0.027 AP.

Both clear the 150-positive threshold from `eval/positive_counts.md` section 5, but the primary clears it by 4 positives. Ablations that were already marginal at 379 positives are not resolvable at 154 - any comparison below the primary threshold is reported as "not resolvable at this sample size", on the primary label, not quietly rerun on the secondary until it resolves.

## 4. Why the primary is the smaller target

The secondary has 2.5x the positives and would produce tighter intervals on every comparison. It is not the headline because a larger sample of the wrong quantity is not worth more than a smaller sample of the right one.

Of the secondary target's 2,955 positives, **1,773 never reached a carrier** - stock unavailability and seller-side cancellation, a different causal generator from a delivery failure and one no checkout-time intervention addresses. A confirmation SMS does not fix a stockout. Ranking those correctly earns PR-AUC that the policy layer cannot convert into avoided cost.

The primary target is narrower, closer to doorstep refusal, and its positives are the ones an intervention could plausibly have changed. Reporting both is what keeps that choice honest: the trade-off is on the page in section 3, quantified, rather than asserted.

## 5. This supersedes a conclusion in `eval/positive_counts.md`

Section 7 of `eval/positive_counts.md` recommends reinstating the EBM ablation (ARCHITECTURE.md sections 5.4, 15) on the grounds that the gate returned a larger test set than estimated. That was computed against 379 all-orders test positives, giving a resolvable gap of ~0.027 AP.

**On the primary target that recommendation no longer holds as written.** At 154 test positives the resolvable gap is ~0.043 AP. Section 5.4 describes the interesting EBM case as a difference of roughly 1 AP point; that was already out of reach at 379 positives and is further out of reach at 154. The ablation is still worth running on the secondary target as a benchmark, but a null result there must be reported as unresolved rather than as evidence the two models are equivalent, and it cannot be transferred to the primary target.

The step 0 artifact is left as written rather than edited after the fact - it records what was true given the gate's own decision rule. This section is the correction.
